Dealing with uncertainty

Supply chains rarely face a single, predictable future. In each situation, you’ll judge which actions are a good or poor response to the uncertainty at hand.

There’s no single right answer for uncertainty — the best response depends on the type of uncertainty and the constraints of the situation. This microtask walks you through three concrete cases and reveals the reasoning behind each action.

Key takeaway

None of the four measures — buffers, flexibility, pooling, or information — is universally the right answer.

The best response always depends on the type of uncertainty and the constraints of the context — perishability, supply availability, cost, and more.

Good supply-chain design means choosing the right combination of measures for the situation at hand. Buffer, flexibility, pooling, and information each answer a different question about uncertainty.

The skill is matching the lever to the constraint — not defaulting to one favourite tool.
